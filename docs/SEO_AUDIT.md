# SEO audit — crawled as Googlebot

**Date:** 2026-08-17
**Method:** live crawl of `poemunity.com` with a Googlebot user-agent — response
headers, server-rendered HTML only (no JS execution), sitemaps parsed, corpus
counted through the public API. No assumptions taken from the code; everything
below is a measurement against production.

---

## Verdict

**The technical SEO is not the problem. It is, honestly, better than most sites
I could crawl. The problem is what the pages contain.**

Corpus composition, counted from `GET /api/v1/poems?origin=…`:

| Origin | Poems | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| `famous` — scraped, seeded from Poetry Foundation | 15,652 | **97.3%** |
| `ai` — generated by the persona simulation | 416 | 2.6% |
| `user` — written by real people | **19** | **0.1%** |
| **Total** | **16,087** | |

A crawler sees a 19,587-URL site whose text is 97% verbatim copies of poems that
exist on poetryfoundation.org and poets.org — the originals, on domains with
twenty years of authority — plus a 2.6% layer of machine-generated verse, plus
nineteen poems that exist nowhere else.

That is the whole explanation for the traffic. Google's coverage report saying
"Discovered – currently not indexed" for the bulk of the site is not a crawling
failure to be fixed with better markup; it is Google having looked and declined
to spend crawl budget. Two published policies describe this corpus directly:
**scaled content abuse** (mass content whose primary value is volume) and
**copied content with no added value**. Both are site-wide signals, which is why
the 19 genuine poems don't rank either — they live on a domain classified by its
other 16,068 pages.

The indexed pages in Search Console being mostly `/authors/` is consistent: those
are the only pages whose *structure* is original, even though the poems they
list are not.

**Separately, and not an SEO matter:** the sample I pulled includes Joseph O.
Legaspi, Dani Couture, Susan Elizabeth Howe and Andrew Shields — living,
in-copyright poets, full texts republished. `seed-poems.js` records the source as
`seed@poetryfoundation.org`. Whatever is decided about rankings, that is standing
legal exposure independent of any of it, and it is the same corpus, so one
decision resolves both.

---

## What is right

Worth stating plainly, because the action plan below asks for content work rather
than technical work, and that is only because the technical work is done.

**Status codes are correct in every case I could construct.**

| URL | Response |
|---|---|
| `www.poemunity.com/` | 308 → apex |
| `/love/` (trailing slash) | 308 → `/love` |
| `/LOVE` | 308 → `/love` |
| `/love?page=9999` | 404 (not a soft 404 over an empty list) |
| `/nonexistent-genre` | 404 |
| `/detail/does-not-exist-xyz` | 404 |
| `/profile` | 307 → `/login` |
| backend unreachable | 503 + `Retry-After` |

- **Canonicals are self-referential and correct**, including on paginated URLs —
  `/love?page=63` canonicalises to itself, not back to page 1.
- **`noindex,follow` is applied precisely** where it should be: `?q=` searches,
  empty genres, origin filters, `/login`, `/register`. Nothing is over-blocked.
- **Titles and descriptions are unique and non-templated**, and paginated titles
  carry the page number (`1247 Love poems — page 2`).
- **Structured data is disciplined.** `WebSite` + `Organization` on the homepage
  only; `CollectionPage` + `BreadcrumbList` on lists; schema.org `Poem` on poem
  pages. No fabricated `Person` for AI personas.
- **Everything is server-rendered.** No JS needed to see content or links.
- **All 3,364 author pages are linked**; max crawl depth to any poem is 5
  (`/` → `/authors` → `?letter=X` → author → `?page=N` → poem).
- **Sitemaps**: an index over four sections, 19,587 URLs, CDN-cached (verified
  `x-vercel-cache: HIT` with rising `age`), and structured so a fetch failure
  500s rather than publishing a partial file.
- **TTFB 0.30–0.49s** across all four page types. Speed is not a constraint.
- `robots.txt`, `lang="en"`, breadcrumbs rendered as well as marked up.

## What is wrong

### 1. The corpus itself — **critical**

Covered above. Everything below this line is worth doing and none of it moves the
needle while this stands.

### 2. Poem pages are thin and are near dead-ends — **high**

Measured on `/detail/mickey-dani`: **145 visible words**, of which the site
chrome (25 genre nav links, footer, headings) accounts for most. Unique poem text
is roughly 40–70 words.

Outbound links from a poem page, deduplicated:

- **1** link to another poem (the Next-poem card)
- 2 to the author, 1 to `/authors`
- 25 to genre pages
- the rest are the footer

So 16,087 poem pages form a **single linked list**: from any poem a crawler can
reach exactly one other poem, and reaching the *n*th costs *n* hops. Poems are
discoverable — via author and genre pages, and the sitemap — but they pass
essentially no link signal to one another, and each one is a leaf.

There is no "More by this author", no "More poems about love", no related-poems
module. Those are the three obvious internal-linking wins on the page type that
makes up 82% of the site.

### 3. Genre pagination strands the middle — **medium**

The nav on `/love?page=63` links: first, last, and ±2. So from page 1 a crawler
reaches pages 1–3 and 125; from 125 it reaches 123–125 and 1. Page 63 is roughly
**30 hops from either end**. With 125 pages per large genre this leaves most
paginated URLs deep even though each one is individually correct.

The poems on them are reachable by other paths, so this is a signal-flow issue
rather than a discovery one — but the paginated URLs themselves are not in the
sitemap either, so nothing shortcuts it.

### 4. Sitemap details — **low**

- `authors.xml` emits **no `<lastmod>` on any of its 3,364 URLs** (the other
  three sections all have it). Google uses `lastmod` for recrawl scheduling.
- All four sections emit `<changefreq>` and `<priority>`. Google has publicly
  ignored both for years. Harmless, but it is 19,587 × 2 lines of noise.
- Paginated list URLs and `?letter=` pages are absent from the sitemap.

### 5. Heading smalls — **low**

- `<h1>` is `Authors` on all 26 letter pages while the `<title>` correctly says
  "Poets starting with B". The H1 should carry the letter.
- `/love?page=2` has `<h1>1247 Love poems</h1>` — identical to page 1, though the
  title does distinguish them.
- `/login` and `/register` have **no `<h1>` at all** (they use `<h2>`). Both are
  `noindex`, so this is accessibility rather than SEO, but it is a one-line fix.

### 6. No page-level edge caching — **low, but it caused the outage**

Every HTML response is `private, no-cache, no-store`, because SSR pages embed
`initialUser`. That is correct as written, and TTFB is fine, so there is no
ranking cost today. It is worth recording only because it is the reason the
crawl surge could overwhelm the backend and produce 1,025 soft 404s — the fix for
which (503 + `Retry-After`) is shipped but not yet validated in Search Console.

## Action plan

Ordered by effect on ranking, not by effort.

### P0 — Decide what this site is

**This is a product decision and it gates everything else.** Three coherent
options; the current state is none of them.

**(a) A community poetry site.** `noindex` the 15,652 famous poems and drop them
from the sitemap; keep them as reader content and as the seed corpus that makes
the site feel inhabited. Indexable surface becomes 435 poems.

⚠️ Note honestly what that leaves: 416 of those 435 are AI-generated. An
indexable corpus that is 96% machine-written is the *same* scaled-content problem
at 1/36th the scale. So (a) is only complete if paired with P1 below —
`noindex` the AI poems too, and accept an indexable corpus of **19 poems plus the
taxonomy**, growing as real people publish.

Small, honest and able to rank. Reversible: it is a flag, not a deletion.

**(b) A poetry archive that adds value.** Keep the famous poems indexed, but make
each page carry something the original doesn't — genuine editorial context,
form/meter notes, curated connections. At 15,652 poems this is not achievable,
and doing it with an LLM is precisely the scaled-content abuse being avoided.
Realistic only for a hand-picked few hundred, which is really option (a) with a
curated exception list.

**(c) Remove the scraped corpus.** Resolves the copyright exposure outright.
Not cheaply reversible, and it would empty the site.

**Recommendation: (a), plus the P1 pairing.** It is the only option that is both
reversible and defensible, and it also settles the copyright question by taking
the pages out of search results.

### P1 — `noindex` the AI-generated poems

Same reasoning, and it is what makes P0(a) actually work. The AI disclosure the
footer and per-poem badges make to *readers* has no equivalent in the index —
Google sees 416 machine-written poems presented like any other. Keep them
visible, keep the badges, keep them out of the index.

Sitemap sections make this cheap: `poems-community.xml` splits into the 19 and
the 416 without new machinery.

### P2 — Make the genre pages the ranking asset

This is the actual opportunity, and it is the one this site can win.

`/love` cannot beat poetryfoundation.org on the text of a John Donne poem. It
*can* compete for **"love poems"**, **"poems about grief"**, **"short friendship
poems"** — navigational, list-intent queries where a well-organised collection is
the right answer and where the ranking pages are usually curated lists, not
primary sources. 136 genre pages, already technically perfect, already indexed.

What they need:

1. **150–300 words of original editorial introduction per genre** — what the form
   or theme is, what to look for, who to read first. Written, not generated. Do
   the 20 largest genres first; that is a weekend, not a quarter.
2. **A curated "start here" selection** above the chronological list.
3. Keep the H1 count, which is already good UX and good for the query.

This is the one item that adds traffic rather than removing liability.

### P3 — Internal linking on poem pages

Three modules, in order of value:

1. **More by this author** (3–5 poems) — the data is already loaded on the page.
2. **More poems in this genre** (3–5) — one extra query.
3. Keep the Next-poem card; it is a good reader feature, just not a link graph.

Turns 16,087 leaf nodes into a connected graph. Do it regardless of the P0
decision — it is a reader improvement first.

### P4 — Pagination reach

Widen the genre nav from ±2 to a fuller window (e.g. 1, 2, …, n−2 … n+2, …,
last−1, last), or add paginated list URLs to `pages.xml`. Either shortens the
30-hop walk to the middle of a large genre.

### P5 — The smalls

One commit, an hour:

- `<lastmod>` on `authors.xml`.
- Drop `<changefreq>` and `<priority>` from all four sections.
- Letter pages: `<h1>Poets starting with B</h1>`.
- Paginated genre H1 carries the page number.
- `<h1>` on `/login` and `/register`.

### P6 — Verify the soft-404 fix

Not new work — the fix shipped in `6218d7a`. In Search Console, hit **Validar
corrección** on the Soft 404 row and watch the 1,025 revalidate. If they come
back as 503 rather than as 200s, that is the fix working, and it points at
edge-caching poem pages to remove the load that caused it.

## What not to do

- **Do not add more scraped or generated poems.** Every one deepens the problem
  P0 exists to undo.
- **Do not write the genre introductions with an LLM.** The distinguishing
  feature of the pages that will rank is that a person wrote them.
- **Do not chase more technical SEO.** It is done. Further markup on a corpus
  Google has classified changes nothing, and the audit above found no technical
  defect worth more than an hour.
- **Do not expect the famous poems to rank if kept.** They are competing against
  the original publisher, on the original publisher's text.

---

## How to measure

The famous/community sitemap split exists precisely for this — submit each child
in Search Console, not just the index, and read coverage per file:

- `poems-famous.xml` — expect indexing to *fall* after P0. That is the intended
  outcome, not a regression.
- `poems-community.xml` — the number that should grow, and the only honest
  measure of whether the site is working.
- `authors.xml` and `pages.xml` — where P2's traffic should appear.

Leading indicator that P0 + P2 worked: **impressions on genre queries**, before
any click growth. If genre-page impressions are still flat 8 weeks after the
editorial work lands, the site-level classification has not lifted yet, and the
answer is time plus more original content — not more technical changes.
